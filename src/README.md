# src: the game

TypeScript, built by Vite into one file, `dist/index.html`. Rules from `CLAUDE.md`: systems change state, rendering only reads it, the UI only sends commands, modules announce events.

| Folder or file | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `core/` | `state.ts` (the saved game), `loop.ts` (fixed 1/30 s step), `events.ts` (event bus), `rng.ts` (seeded randomness), `clock.ts` (time of day, 12-minute days), `save.ts` (versioned saves with migrations) |
| `data/` | Tables only: `skills.ts` (skills, resources and their models, items, from the Wanderskill prototype; the tools each swing needs), `xp.ts` (the 1-99 curve), `animation.ts` (each skill's swing: clip, tool, timings and standing distance, from `assets/models/miner.json`), `hauling.ts` (backpack size), `changelog.ts` (what changed in each version, in players' words, for the "What's new" note), `goals.ts` (the goals that lead a new player through the camp; gathering goals and making goals), `making.ts` (things he makes: what each needs, where it is made, how long it takes, what it gives: a structure such as the campfire, or a tool such as the stone axe), `pickups.ts` (things picked up by hand: what each gives, its model, whether it comes back, and which group tapping it gathers) |
| `world/layout.ts` | The village (VILLAGE), in rings from the square: buildings (each kind has a ring in `RING`), props, gardens, rocks and trees to work (nodes), scenery trees, lanterns, river, roads and lanes, the land under fog; where things are built (`siteOf`) |
| `world/camp.ts` | Where a new game starts (CAMP): a clearing below a waterfall pouring down a towering wall, its trees and rocks, the wreck, stones and branches to pick up, the fire spot where the campfire is built and the tent's spot, the river and the falls |
| `world/water.ts` | The water as one shape: the river (a smooth channel with gently wavy banks, running on past the haze) and the camp's pool (an irregular basin opening into it); how far a point is from the water's edge, for drawing, walking and keeping trees off the bank |
| `world/falls.ts` | The foot of the camp's wall: where it stands, the scree slope against it, and the rocks (boulders, smaller rocks, rubble) and ferns on and below it; the bigger rocks block walking |
| `world/nav.ts` | Walking: a grid of where a person can stand (the water and the bigger rocks at the wall's foot block; things to pick up and rubble don't), A* paths and straight-line smoothing; everything that blocks goes here, what he has built too (one grid per set of built things) |
| `actors/player.ts` | Walking to a tap, to a rock or tree (stand within the tool's reach), to a building's door or the edge of unclaimed land, to stand beside something to pick up, or beside where he builds |
| `systems/` | `gathering.ts` (swings, yields into the backpack, rocks and trees running out and growing back, moving on), `backpack.ts` (the backpack and the bank), `hauling.ts` (a full backpack walked to the bank and back to work or gathering; tapping the bank), `skills.ts` (XP, level-ups), `pickups.ts` (picking things up by hand, the book, stones and branches coming back; gathering: a tapped plank, stone or branch leads on to the nearest of its group that fits in the backpack, waiting for stones and branches to come back), `making.ts` (making things: he works beside the site or where he stands, then the needs are taken from the backpack, then the bank, and a structure stands or he owns a tool), `goals.ts` (writes finished goals down, so they stay finished), `commands.ts` (the UI's only way in; refuses a tree or rock without its tool, and making something at the camp before the goals before it are done, or without everything it needs, saying what comes first or what's missing; a stone or branch tapped with a full backpack goes to the bank first where there is one) |
| `sim.ts` | One simulation step; headless, used by the game and the tests |
| `render/` | three.js: `view.ts` (the character and his tools, at the camp the axe drawn as the stone axe, a placeholder bow while he builds or makes something, the click cross), `arms.ts` (whether the tool is in his hands or on his back, and keeps his hands on the handle when clips crossfade), `nodes.ts` (rocks and trees: running out, falling, stumps, growing back, chips and leaves), `waterfall.ts` (the camp's towering wall lost in mist and cloud, falling water, foam and spray, and the foot of the mountain: scree slope, rocks, moss and ferns; how far a point is in front of the wall, for the camera), `structures.ts` (what he builds: the campfire with flickering flames, smoke and a warm light; until then the fire spot's faint ring, filling up while he builds), `pickups.ts` (things to pick up: hidden once taken, a glint to spot them, a faint one inside a ring that fills up where he waits for a stone or branch to come back), `world.ts` (ground, paths, the sandy bank and stony ground at the wall's foot, the river and pool as one sheet of water, running on out of sight with its own sand bank past the detailed ground), `village.ts` (buildings, trees, lanterns, hearth, night glow, merged per material), `fog.ts` (mist over unclaimed land), `lighting.ts` (day and night), `camera.ts` (third-person orbit; never goes behind the camp's wall) |
| `ui/` | `input.ts` (tap, drag, pinch, keys), `hud.ts` (panels, floating numbers, the goal card and its Build button), `news.ts` ("What's new" after an update; the version button), `intro.ts` (the welcome for a new player), `backpack.ts` (what's in the backpack, opened from the panel, with buttons to drop things) |
| `main.ts` | Wires it all together; `window.hearthfolk` exposes state and view for checking in the browser console |

Tests are in `tests/`; the long headless run in `tests/play.test.ts` is the start of the plan's multi-day simulation check. `tests/architecture.test.ts` enforces the module boundaries: each folder may only import from the layers listed there, the simulation layers may not touch three.js or the page, and `render/` and `ui/` may not assign to game state. If it fails, move the code to the right layer rather than loosening the rule.
