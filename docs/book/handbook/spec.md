# Making Do: print and game spec

The printed-handbook direction for Hearthfolk's survival book. One design for two places: a pocket book a printer can make in a short run, and the same pages read one at a time on a phone. Mock-up: `index.html` in this folder. Text: `copy.md`.

## In one line

A 120 × 180 mm, 64-page, section-sewn softcover on cream uncoated paper, printed inside in two solid inks (black and copper-rust), with a cream board cover printed forest green, matt laminated, one copper foil block and rounded corners.

## Trim size: 120 × 180 mm, portrait

- **Pocket-sized.** It goes in a jacket or cargo pocket, like a real field manual, and still fits a 94 mm wide figure and 10 pt text.
- **2:3, close to the phone's shape.** The same parts sit in the same order on a phone page with little rearranging. The game re-sets the page rather than scaling the print page (see "How the game shows it").
- **Easy to print.** Short-run digital and offset printers cut any size; 120 × 180 mm sits well on standard press sheets. If print-on-demand is ever needed instead, use 5 × 8 in (127 × 203 mm) with the same layout and the extra height added to the top and bottom margins.

## Page count: 64

- **Four sewn sections of 16 pages.** One press sheet folded four times makes a section, so 64 wastes no paper and binds the ordinary way.
- **Every goal is a spread.** Chapters always start on an even (left) page, so each job lies open in front of you and never needs a page turn mid-task.
- **24 chapters fit.** That is a working guess for the first act of the game (camp, road, village). If the game has more goals when it's printed, go to **80 pages** (five sections, up to 32 chapters). Never add pages in ones or twos: always a whole section of 16.

## Margins, bleed and grid

| | Size | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bleed | 3 mm on every edge, cover included | Standard for every printer |
| Safe zone | 5 mm inside the trim | Nothing important closer to the edge |
| Inner (gutter) | 15 mm | A sewn book opens well, but the gutter still curves |
| Outer | 11 mm | Room for the thumb tab and thumbs |
| Top | 12 mm | Running head sits 5 mm from the top edge |
| Bottom | 16 mm | Folio sits 6.5 mm from the bottom edge |
| Text block | 94 × 152 mm | |

- **Thumb tab:** a plain copper-rust block on the outer edge, 6 mm inside the trim plus 3 mm bleed (9 mm in the file), 20 mm tall. **No word on it:** the running head already names the part, and a word that close to the trim would sit unevenly once normal trimming drift (up to 1 mm) is added. Part One sits 40 mm from the top, Part Two 72 mm, Part Three 104 mm, so the parts can be found by the edge of the book.
- **Type sizes (at the printed size):** body Literata 10/14 pt; legend, rule box and captions 8.25/11.5 pt; footnotes and small labels 7.25 pt; labels Barlow Condensed 7.5 pt, letterspaced 10%; story line Literata italic 11/15.5 pt; chapter titles Fraunces 23.5 pt; the chapter number 43 pt reversed out of a 20 mm copper-rust square.
- **Hierarchy without tints:** every word prints in 100% black (or 100% copper-rust for labels, chapter numbers and callouts). Story lines, captions and footnotes are set apart by italic, size and weight, never by a grey. A screened grey turns 7 pt italic into dots on uncoated paper.
- **Figures:** full text-block width (94 mm). All lines 100% black: main lines 0.32 mm, detail lines 0.15–0.2 mm, nothing thinner than 0.12 mm. Detail is lighter because it is thinner, not because it is grey. Dark areas are hatched, not tinted. Figures carry only letters (A, B, C, D) in circles; all the words sit in the legend beneath, so text can be edited or translated without touching the art.
- **Capitals:** labels are title case and letterspaced, never all capitals, matching the game's rule. The only capitals are on the round emblem stamp, because stamps are like that.

## Paper, binding and finishing

- **Text paper:** Munken Premium Cream, 100 gsm (or the printer's own 90–100 gsm cream uncoated book paper). Warm, opaque enough that figures don't show through, and nice under pencil. The page colour is the paper's own; never print a paper texture.
- **Cover board:** 300 gsm uncoated board in **cream/natural** (not white), grain **parallel to the spine**. The cream type on the front and back is not ink: it is the board showing through the green, and the inside of the cover is the board's own cream with green type, so the colour must come from the board to match the mock-up.
- **Cover print:** outside in CMYK: the forest-green flood with a fine printed grain, the tide-mark and the reversed-out type. Keep the green's total ink at or under 260% (about C75 M40 Y100 K45). Inside in one colour (green) for the Rules and the ruler.
- **Matt laminate, outside only.** Cheap and standard. A full green flood on uncoated board cracks white at the spine and hinge scores and scuffs in a pocket; the laminate stops both and gives the soft, cloth-like hand.
- **Order of work:** print, matt laminate, then (optional) an all-over linen emboss, then the copper foil, then score, bind and round the corners. Foil on a textured surface breaks up fine detail, so **every foil detail is sized to survive it:** strokes 0.4 mm or more, and the emblem's ring lettering at 3 mm cap height or more (in the mock it is 3.0 mm with about 0.5 mm stems, and the picture inside the ring is drawn smaller to clear it). If the hard proof shows the ring lettering filling in, take it off the foil die and keep it only in ink on the title page.
- **Scores:** at the spine and 6 mm in from each spine fold, so it opens without cracking.
- **Binding:** section-sewn (Smyth-sewn) softcover. It lies open on a rock, survives being used and outlives a glued spine. A hardcover would cost two to three times as much and stop being pocket-sized.
- **Foil:** one copper foil block: the title, emblem, series line and publisher on the front, and the title on the spine. One die, one pass.
- **Corners:** all four outer corners rounded to a 6 mm radius after binding (one pass on a corner rounder). It is the single cheapest thing that makes it look like a field manual.
- **Spine:** about 5 mm for 64 pages; get the exact figure from the printer's spine calculator before the cover art is finished. The spine carries "Making Do" and "7" in foil.
- **Inks inside: two solid inks, black and copper-rust.** On a digital short run (a few hundred to about 2,000 copies) both print from CMYK; on a big offset run, black plus one Pantone matched to the copper-rust swatch at proof stage. **All type and line art is 100% of one ink.** Tints are used only for fills, and only these:
  - **Rule box:** 13% copper-rust (12–15% is the safe band; under 10% nearly vanishes on uncoated paper).
  - **Tide-mark (inside):** a named tint of copper-rust, "Tide": 7% for the body of the stain, 18% for its dried edge, which is drawn as a band at least 1.5 mm wide, never as a fine line.
  - **Fire in the figures:** 14% copper-rust fill inside a solid copper-rust outline.
  - **Field Notes rules:** solid copper-rust at 0.1 mm (a thin solid line, not a tint).
- **The water damage is printed.** A tide-mark on the cover's lower edge (back, spine and front, as one stain) and a faint one on the lower outer corners inside that shrinks chapter by chapter, because the book went in front first. A clean "shop edition" just drops those layers.
- **Before the run:** final files as PDF/X-4 with 3 mm bleed, fonts embedded, figures as vectors; one hard proof checked for the copper-rust, the two tints and the foil on the laminate.

## How the game shows it

The same chapter record drives both: number, title, story, teaches, tools, parts with quantities, steps (and which ones count), figure, legend, done when, rule and footnote. The print layout and the game both read it, so the two never drift apart.

**One rule for every screen:** the game lays the page out at its own sizes and never scales the print page. Text size comes from the device and the player's text-size setting, never from the window's height. Everything is sized in em from one root size: 16 px on phones, tablets and PCs, 26 px on a TV (the 24–28 px range for reading from a sofa).

- **Game sizes (at the 16 px root):** body, story line, legend, rule text, "Done when" and Teaches/Tools all 16 px; chapter titles 23 px; footnote 14.5 px; Barlow labels 13 px. Nothing a player reads is under 14.5 px except the 13 px labels.
- **Figures have a game version** drawn from the same art: every stroke 1.4 times thicker, callout circles about 11–12 px radius with 14–15 px letters, labels 12.5 px or more, detail lines in the soft screen colour.
- **Phones (portrait): one page per screen,** with 16 px gutters. No running head and no folio on the page: the page number is in the bottom bar's label ("Chapter 2 · page 6"). **The design target is a page area about 480 px tall** at a 300 px measure (a 360 × 640 phone gives 328 × 496 px; a 375 × 667 phone 343 × 523; a 390 × 844 phone 358 × 647). Within it, the job page (everything except "Done when") fits on one screen, and so does the figure with its legend. Measured in the mock-up, Chapter 3's job page, the longest of the three, needs 465 px against 466 available at 360 × 640.
- **A spread becomes two screens:** the job page first, then the picture page. On phones "Done when" moves to the job page when it fits there, so one screen says what to do and when it's finished.
- **When a page won't fit, it splits between whole blocks, never mid-line.** The rest moves to a second screen, marked "Continued ›" at the foot of the first and "Page 7, continued" at the top of the second, and the bar label reads "page 7 · 1 of 2". "Done when" goes back beside the rule, where print has it. On a 375 × 667 or 360 × 640 phone a chapter is three screens: the job; the figure and legend; then "Done when", the rule and the footnote. On a 390 × 844 phone Chapters 1 and 2 take two screens; Chapter 3's longer legend and rule split its picture page even there.
- **If even one block won't fit** (a big text setting, or a phone browser with its toolbars showing), that page scrolls inside itself with a thin, visible copper scrollbar, a fade at the foot and a "More below" chip that goes once the end is reached. Text is never hidden without a cue.
- **Wide screens (tablets held sideways, PCs, TVs): two game pages side by side,** the job on the left and the picture on the right, each laid out exactly like the phone page at the game sizes above, with the page width capped at 30 em (480 px on a PC, 780 px on a TV) so lines stay a comfortable length. A 1080p TV fits two 780 px pages with room to spare and a page area of about 33 em tall, more than the 30 em a page needs.
- **When two pages won't fit, show one at a time.** If the window is too narrow for two pages, or too short to hold the taller page at body text of 15 px or more without splitting, the game shows one page at a time like a phone (which can then be up to 34 em wide, so it needs less height), with the same split and scroll rules.
- **Opening it:** tapping the goal card, or the book in the pack, opens the book at the current chapter's job page.
- **Controls are game interface,** forest green panels with cream text: × to close (top right), ‹ and › to turn (bottom), a page label between them. Swipe, ← →, a controller's D-pad or shoulder buttons turn pages; Esc or B closes. Tap targets are at least 44 px.
- **The pencil layer:** the game draws ticks, counts such as 2/4 and the player's name in pencil (Caveat, #5E5A55). The printed book leaves all of it blank for its owner, except Chapter 1's first box ("Find this book"), which is printed already ticked.
- **Stuck pages:** chapters past the current one are stuck together from the river. Turning past the current chapter shows the stuck block and a game chip explaining it. Finishing a goal unsticks the next spread (a short peel) and offers to turn to it. The book never spoils what's next.
- **Water damage:** the game copy carries the same tide-marks as print, strongest at the front. On phones, bake the paper grain and stains into a tiling image rather than live SVG filters.
