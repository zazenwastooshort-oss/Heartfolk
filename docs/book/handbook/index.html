Making Do

Most people buy this book before they need it. A few find it afterwards, on a riverbank, slightly damp.

Either way, Making Do starts where you are, with nothing, and gets you to a fire, a roof and a dry box to keep things in, one plain job at a time. Every chapter tells you what you need, what to do and what it should look like. None asks for anything you can't find or make by then.

How to Use This Book

Each chapter is one job, set across two facing pages so the book can lie open while you work. Start at Chapter 1. No chapter needs anything from a later one.

  1. AChapter number and name
  2. ESteps. Tick each one off
  3. BWhat's going on
  4. FThe figure, parts lettered
  5. CWhat it teaches; tools needed
  6. GThe rule. Remember it
  7. DYou will need: exact amounts
  8. HFootnotes. Read these too
  • Quantities are exact. Four stones means four. Three won't do, and five is showing off.
  • Tick in pencil. Ink runs, and you've seen what water does.
  • Later pages may be stuck together. Don't force them. They'll come apart when you get there.

Chapters 1 to 3

Every goal is one spread. Left page, the job: the chapter, a line of story, what it teaches, what you need and the steps to tick. Right page, the picture: a lettered figure, the rule, and a footnote. The thumb tab on the outer edge marks the part, so the book can be opened by feel.

In the game

Chapter 2 on a phone, as the player sees it with the campfire half gathered. One page per screen, re-set at 16 px rather than shrunk. Turn the pages with the arrows, a swipe or the arrow keys; close it with the ×. Switch the screen size to see how a short phone handles a page that won't fit.

Your book

  • Opens at the job in handTapping the goal card, or the book in the pack, opens it at the current chapter's left page: what's needed and how far along you are.
  • Same page, set for the handThe printed page is re-set, not shrunk: body text, legends and the rule at 16 px, one page per screen, no running head (the page number is in the bar). A printed spread becomes two screens, the job first and then the picture.
  • Short screens split, never cutPages are designed for a page area about 480 px tall. If one still won't fit, the game moves whole blocks to a second screen marked "Continued", and "Done when" goes back beside the rule, as in print. If even one block won't fit, the page scrolls, with a thin scrollbar and a "More below" cue.
  • The pencil is the game'sTicks, counts like 2/4 and the player's name are pencilled in by the game. Printed copies leave them blank for their owner.
  • Pages ahead are stuck togetherThe river glued the rest of the book shut. Each chapter comes apart when you reach it, so the book never gives away what's next.
  • Game controls, book pagesThe buttons are ordinary game interface, forest green with cream. The book itself keeps its paper and ink on every screen. Arrows, swipe, ← →, a controller's D-pad; close with ×, Esc or B.
  • Wide screens get two pagesTablets held sideways, PCs and TVs show the job and the picture side by side, each laid out exactly like the phone page at the game's own sizes: 16 px body, 26 px on a TV. Type never shrinks to fit a window; when two pages won't fit, it shows one at a time.
