Two ways to make the survival book

The book is in the game from the first minute: its pages are your goals, in order. It also has to work as a real printed book, maybe as a Kickstarter reward. Two designers each made one direction, and each was reviewed for phone readability and for print, then polished. Open both, then tell me which one, or which parts of each.

Direction B · the field journal

A Field Guide to Having Nothing

Ida Fenn's leather notebook. She went over the same falls years ago and wrote down how she got by. She gave it to her grandson, your character. Her ink is the guide; his pencil notes appear as you finish pages.

"Day nine. A fire, a roof, an axe, a pick and a box. That's not nothing, Nan."His pencil note when page 7 is done

Each goal
One spread. Left: Ida's entry with boxes to tick and who taught her. Right: her pencil sketch, sometimes with a note taped in.
Feel
Personal and full of story. It brings people with it: Os the charcoal burner, Gret the miner, who could turn up later in the game.
In the game
One page per screen in handwriting at 22 px. Tap a sketch to see it big. An easy-read setting swaps the handwriting for print.
Printing
Hardcover notebook, 96 pages, full colour, elastic band and ribbon. Dearer to make, and more of a keepsake.

Two things both books ask of the game

  1. Seven planks, not six

    Both books count 7 planks: 5 loose and 2 from the smashed crate, which is exactly what lies on the shore. 3 go into the fire and 4 into the shelter. The game's first goal asks for 6 right now. I'd change it to 7.

  2. What the fire burns

    They disagree here. Making Do has you put 10 logs on the fire. The journal says the fire runs on deadwood he picks up without being asked, so your logs go into the shelter instead. This is the open question about who feeds the fire.
