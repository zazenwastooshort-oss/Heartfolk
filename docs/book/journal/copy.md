# The Field Journal: final copy

The book is a weathered field journal kept by **Ida Fenn**, the character's grandmother. Years ago she went over the same falls with nothing and lived to write down how she got by, and who taught her each thing. She gave the book to her grandson. Now he has gone over the falls too, with her book in his pocket.

Who writes what:

- **Ida's ink** (Caveat, sepia black): the entries. She is the guide's voice: warm, practical, dry.
- **The grandson's pencil** (Patrick Hand, graphite): short notes at the foot of each entry. In the game each note writes itself in when the player finishes that page. The printed edition prints them all.
- **Other people's hands** (Kalam): notes taped in or written straight into the book by the people Ida learned from. Os writes in charcoal on brown paper; Gret writes a whole entry (No. 6) in blue-black ink. Later entries in the game continue this way: the smith, the miller and others write their own pages.

The printed form on every page (forest green, Fraunces Italic) has three fields: **No.**, **Place**, **Weather**. Ida fills them in. On sketch pages she writes the number again and ditto marks (”) in the other two.

"Teaches" lines are notes for the game team. They are not printed or shown in the book.

Every material line has a where-to-find hint in italics after it. Sketch labels come in two sets: the printed labels, and shorter **phone labels** that the game sets in HTML at 22 px over a line-only drawing. Tapping a sketch in the game shows the printed drawing, with the printed labels, full size.

---

## Title options

1. **A Field Guide to Having Nothing** (my pick)
2. Start With Fire
3. Notes from the Bottom of the Falls

**Why the first:** it tells the joke at once (a survival handbook for someone who has nothing at all), it is plain and warm rather than grim, and the first chapter pays it off: on page 7 the grandson writes "That's not nothing, Nan." *Start With Fire* is strong but sounds like any bushcraft book. *Notes from the Bottom of the Falls* is true to the opening but sadder than the game is.

## The entries

Each entry is one spread: the left page is the entry (everything the game needs), the right page is the sketch.

### No. 1 · Salvage the wreck (pages 4-5)

- **Place:** The pool below the falls
- **Weather:** Wet. Very.

**Story:** If you're reading this, you've been in the river. So was I. Before you do anything clever, sit up, count your fingers and gather what came down with you. It isn't much, but it's more than nothing.

**Pick up**
- ☑ This book *(there, one done already)* — Ida ticked this box herself, in ink. It starts ticked in the game too.
- ☐ The planks *along the shore. All of them: there should be 7.*
- ☐ The tarp *on the rocks by the pool. Torn, but take it.*

**Remember:** Pick up everything. Out here, nothing that floats is rubbish.

**Aside:** Don't wade out after anything the river's still holding. It's had its turn.

**Learned from:** the river, the hard way.

**Pencil (when done):** Nan. I'm at the bottom of the falls too. Runs in the family.

**Sketch (page 5): "What came down with me".** A flat lay, like a kit list: the tarp spread out (torn corner, grommets), seven planks in a stack, the crate in bits, the book.
Labels: *the tarp. Torn, but it'll do.* · *7 planks. 3 for a fire, 4 for a roof. Count them.* · *the crate, in bits. Kindling now.* · *and this book. (well done)*
Taped in: a torn shipping label off the crate, printed THIS WAY UP with two arrows. Ida, beside it: *It wasn't.*
Phone labels (22 px, set over the drawing): *the tarp. Torn, but it'll do.* · *7 planks. 3 for a fire, 4 for a roof.* · *Count them.* (beside the stack) · *the crate, in bits. Kindling now.* · *and this book. (well done)* · *It wasn't.*

**Teaches:** tap a thing on the ground and he walks over and picks it up into the backpack. The first box starts ticked, so the player sees how ticking works before doing anything.

### No. 2 · A fire for the night (pages 6-7)

- **Place:** Same rock as yesterday
- **Weather:** Cold, then colder

**Story:** The first night is the worst one. A fire won't dry you, feed you or tell you where you are, but it makes all three tomorrow's problem.

**You'll need**
- ☐ **3 planks** *from the wreck*
- ☐ **4 stones** *off the shore, by hand*

**Then**
- ☐ Build it, out of the wind

**Remember:** Fire first. Everything goes better warm.

**Aside:** Not too near the tarp. Ask me how I know. *(the page has a singed corner)*

**Learned from:** my dad, who said it every single time.

**Pencil (when done):** Night one. Lit it. Took forty minutes and some words Nan wouldn't print.

**Sketch (page 7): "The fire, side on".** A ring of four stones, three planks leaned into a cone, the fire's back to a big rock, smoke curling up.
Labels: *wind* · *back it onto a rock, out of the wind* · *3 planks lean in, like friends* · *4 stones, in a ring* · *3 big paces from the tarp*
Insets: *from above* (the four stones in a ring, planks meeting in the middle) · *strike two stones low, into a nest of tarp threads* (two stones, sparks, a tuft of threads)
Phone labels: *wind* · *back it onto a rock, out of the wind* · *3 planks lean in, like friends* · *4 stones, in a ring* · *3 big paces from the tarp* · *from above* · *strike two stones low over tarp threads*

**Teaches:** gather by hand (stones lie on the shore, tap to pick up), then build from what you carry. Building happens where he stands.

### No. 3 · A sharp edge (pages 8-9)

- **Place:** The shingle bank
- **Weather:** Dry, for once

**Story:** Bare hands only get you so far. Two branches and two stones make an axe, and an axe makes nearly everything else.

**You'll need**
- ☐ **2 branches** *fallen ones, under the trees. One for the handle, one to break while you work out how.*
- ☐ **2 stones** *off the shingle: a hard flat one, and one to knock it sharp*

**Then**
- ☐ Make the stone axe

**Remember:** Make the tool first. A tool turns a day of work into an hour.

**Aside:** It won't be pretty. Mine looked like a dog had been at it. It still worked.

**Learned from:** Os, a charcoal burner, for the price of a pie. He ate the pie first.

**Pencil (when done):** Second branch broke. Exactly as promised.

**Sketch (page 9): "Stone axe, in three goes".**
1. *split the end* (a branch with its end split)
2. *knock one sharp with the other* (a hammer stone striking a flat stone, chips flying)
3. *wedge it in, bind it tight (a strip off the tarp)* (the finished axe)
Also: *sharp edge out* · *hold it down here* · *the spare. (you'll see)* (a branch snapped in two)
Taped in, Os's own note, in charcoal on brown paper: *Split it, wedge it, bind it wet. It shrinks tight as it dries. Mind your thumb. — Os*
Phone labels: *1. split the end* · *2. knock one sharp with the other* · *3. wedge it in, bind it tight with a tarp strip* · *sharp edge out* · *hold it down here* · *the spare. (you'll see)*. On a phone, Os's note sits under the drawing, not on it.

**Teaches:** tools open new work. With the axe he can chop trees (Woodcutting).

### No. 4 · Firewood (pages 10-11)

- **Place:** The birches by the pool
- **Weather:** Turning

**Story:** A fire eats more than you'd think and never says thank you. Keep a pile beside it, and keep the pile bigger than you think you need.

**You'll need**
- ☐ **The stone axe** *(you made it. Well done again.)*

**Then**
- ☐ Chop **10 birch logs** *the white ones by the pool. Stack them by the fire, off the ground.*

**Remember:** The more you chop, the better you get. Your arms will tell you about it first.

**Aside:** Birch burns even when it's damp, which is the kindest thing a tree has ever done for me.

**Learned from:** my own two arms, mostly.

**Pencil (when done):** Ten. Can't lift a cup. Worth it.

**Sketch (page 11): "Birch, and how to drop one".** A birch with its cut, a log stack.
Labels: *white bark: birch. Burns wet.* · *notch on the side you want it to fall* · *stand here, not there* · *stack it off the ground, bark up*
Phone labels: *white bark: birch. Burns wet.* · *notch the side it should fall* · *stand here, not there* · *stack it off the ground*

**Teaches:** repeat work to level a skill (Woodcutting XP and levels). Logs go in the backpack and stay there: the campfire never takes logs from the backpack (see the note at the end).

### No. 5 · A roof (pages 12-13)

- **Place:** Under the cliff
- **Weather:** Rain, sideways

**Story:** Sleeping under the sky is lovely in songs. Out here it's mostly damp. Get a roof up before the rain finds out where you live.

**You'll need**
- ☐ **The tarp** *from the wreck, torn corner and all*
- ☐ **4 planks** *the other 4 from the wreck*
- ☐ **10 logs** *the pile from No. 4. The fire can make do with deadwood.*

**Then**
- ☐ Put up the shelter

**Remember:** With a roof, it's a camp. Without one, it's just where you landed.

**Aside:** Slope it so the rain runs off behind you, not down your neck.

**Learned from:** a shepherd on the high moor, who had slept out in worse and liked to say so.

**Pencil (when done):** Hole in the tarp is right over my feet. Fine. Feet are fine.

**Sketch (page 13): "A lean-to against the cliff".**
Labels: *logs for the frame* · *planks for a floor, off the wet* · *tarp over, sloped to the back* · *mind the torn corner*
Phone labels: *logs for the frame* · *planks for a floor* · *tarp over, sloped back* · *mind the torn corner*

**Teaches:** building changes the camp; the shelter is home base (where he rests).

### No. 6 · Breaking rock (pages 14-15)

Written in Gret's hand (Kalam, blue-black ink), straight into Ida's book.

At the top: *Ida asked me to write this one so it's done properly. — G.*

- **Place:** Any rock with green in it
- **Weather:** Never matters underground

**Story:** Rock doesn't care how hard you hit it, only where. Make a pick, find the stone with green streaks in it, and hit it where it's already cracked.

**You'll need**
- ☐ **2 branches** *under the birches. One for the handle, one spare. You know why.*
- ☐ **3 stones** *the shingle. Two for the points, one to knock them sharp.*

**Then**
- ☐ Make the stone pickaxe
- ☐ Mine **3 copper ore** *the green-streaked rock*

**Remember:** Every new tool opens a new kind of work. A pick opens everything under your feet.

**Aside:** Green streaks mean copper. Shiny yellow means you're about to be disappointed.

**Learned from:** me. Gret Tarrow, forty years in Copperhead pit.

**Ida, in the margin (ink):** She's not wrong. She's never wrong. It's exhausting. — I.

**Pencil (when done):** Green stone, cracked side. She's not wrong.

**Sketch (page 15): "Where to hit it".** A double-pointed stone pick, a rock face with cracks and green streaks.
Labels: *green = copper* · *hit here, along the crack* · *not here. You'll just bounce.*
Phone labels: *green = copper* · *hit here, along the crack* · *not here. You'll bounce.*

**Teaches:** a new tool opens a new skill (Mining). First ore.

### No. 7 · Keeping things dry (pages 16-17)

Back in Ida's hand.

- **Place:** Back at camp
- **Weather:** Fair, for now

**Story:** Things left on the ground get wet, get lost or get eaten. A chest with a lid keeps all three off, and gives you somewhere to put more than you can carry.

**You'll need**
- ☐ **10 more logs** *back to the birches. Your arms know the way.*
- ☐ **3 copper ore** *the ore from No. 6. Pick out the bright bits and hammer them flat for hinges.*

**Then**
- ☐ Build the storage chest

**Remember:** Once you've somewhere to keep things, you can start having things.

**Aside:** Lid on, always. The squirrels watch you as closely as you watch them.

**Learned from:** a squirrel. Honestly. Watch one for an afternoon.

**Pencil (when done):** Day nine. A fire, a roof, an axe, a pick and a box. That's not nothing, Nan.

**Sketch (page 17): "A chest, and who it's for".** A log-and-plank chest with copper strap hinges and a hasp; a squirrel on a stump, watching.
Labels: *copper bits, hammered flat* · *lid on, always* · *him*
Phone labels: the same three.

**Teaches:** storage. The backpack empties into the chest, and what's in the chest is safe (the first bank).

## Numbers for the game

The brief gave most quantities; the rest are chosen so every page adds up, and every material line in the book says where to get it.

- **Planks in the wreck: 7** (3 for the campfire + 4 for the shelter). Page 5 says "count them".
- **The campfire never takes logs from the backpack.** It runs on deadwood he picks up without being asked (not counted, never shown as an item). So No. 4's 10 logs are still in the backpack for No. 5's frame ("the pile from No. 4"), and No. 7 sends the player back to the birches for 10 more. Logs chopped in total by No. 7: 20.
- **Stone pickaxe: 2 branches + 3 stones** (an axe is 2 + 2; a pick has two points, so one stone more).
- **Copper ore mined in No. 6: 3**, and **the chest takes 10 logs + 3 copper ore**, so the first ore all goes into the chest's hinges.
