A Field Guide to Having Nothing

A weathered leather notebook kept by Ida Fenn, who went over the same falls years ago with nothing and wrote down how she got by, and who taught her each thing. She gave it to her grandson. Now he has gone over too, with her book in his pocket. Her ink is the guide; his pencil notes fill in as you play; other people's notes are taped in where they said it better.

How to use this book

  1. Do the pages in order. Each one leans on the one before.
  2. Tick the boxes as you go. It won't make the work lighter, but you'll feel better.
  3. Read who taught me. If you ever meet them, say thank you. Some of them will want a pie.
  4. Write in it. It's a notebook, not a prayer book.
  5. Keep it dry. (I didn't, twice. You can see where.)

No. 1 · Salvage the wreck pages 4–5

Every entry is one spread. Left: the entry, with everything the game needs (story, what to pick up, the boxes to tick, the lesson, who taught her). Right: the sketch. Ida ticked the first box herself, so the player's first page starts with one done.

1
PlaceThe pool below the falls
WeatherWet. Very.

Salvage the wreck

If you're reading this, you've been in the river. So was I. Before you do anything clever, sit up, count your fingers and gather what came down with you. It isn't much, but it's more than nothing.

Pick up

  • This book (there, one done already)
  • The planks along the shore. All of them: there should be 7.
  • The tarp on the rocks by the pool. Torn, but take it.

Remember: Pick up everything. Out here, nothing that floats is rubbish.

Don't wade out after anything the river's still holding. It's had its turn.

Learned from: the river, the hard way.

Nan. I'm at the bottom of the falls too. Runs in the family.

A fire for the night

The first night is the worst one. A fire won't dry you, feed you or tell you where you are, but it makes all three tomorrow's problem.

You'll need

  • 3 planks from the wreck
  • 4 stones off the shore, by hand

Then

  • Build it, out of the wind

Remember: Fire first. Everything goes better warm.

Not too near the tarp. Ask me how I know.

No. 3 · A sharp edge pages 8–9

The book records where each piece of know-how came from. Here Os, the charcoal burner who taught her the axe, has his own note taped in, written in charcoal (what else) on brown paper.

3
PlaceThe shingle bank
WeatherDry, for once

A sharp edge

Bare hands only get you so far. Two branches and two stones make an axe, and an axe makes nearly everything else.

You'll need

  • 2 branches fallen ones, under the trees. One for the handle, one to break while you work out how.
  • 2 stones off the shingle: a hard flat one, and one to knock it sharp

Then

  • Make the stone axe

Remember: Make the tool first. A tool turns a day of work into an hour.

It won't be pretty. Mine looked like a dog had been at it. It still worked.

Learned from: Os, a charcoal burner, for the price of a pie. He ate the pie first.

Second branch broke. Exactly as promised.

  • Opens on the current page. Picking up the book or tapping the book button lands on the entry for the goal you're on.
  • One page per screen. The entry first (everything you need to play), then its sketch. Swipe or use the arrows; the gutter shadow stays on the side it has in the printed book.
  • Try it: tap a box. That is what the game does when you finish an item. Tick all three on No. 2 and his pencil note writes itself in on the blank line kept for it under the boxes, as No. 1's already has. If the line is off screen, the page scrolls to it first.
  • More below? When a page runs past the screen, the foot of the page fades and a small pencil chevron shows. Tap it, or scroll.
  • Tap a sketch to see the whole printed drawing, big. On the page, its labels are set at 22 px over the drawing, in fewer words.
  • Pages after the next one are stuck together from the river. They come apart as you go, so you can always read one page ahead and no further.
  • Same words, same order as the printed book. Type is set for the phone: Ida's hand at 22 px (Caveat runs small, so that reads like about 15.5 px of an ordinary font), her hints and asides at 21 px, his pencil at 17.5 px.
  • On wide screens the game shows the exact printed spread only when a page comes out at least 820 px tall. Smaller screens get two phone pages side by side, and a TV gets them with Ida at 34 px.
